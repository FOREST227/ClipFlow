## Design Context

### Users
Mac users who repeatedly copy text, code, links and images between apps. The main job is to find and reuse a recent item quickly while keeping sensitive content controlled.

### Brand Personality
Native, compact, reassuring. Follow the established positioning in docs/clipflow-design.md: Spotlight, Control Center and Quick Look rather than a dashboard.

### Aesthetic Direction
Preserve the existing macOS typography, light/dark appearance and category accents. The user prefers the original richer visual identity: warm-to-blue gradients, brand icons, status summaries, colorful category cards and content labels. Keep the newer page structure and scrolling behavior while restoring these elements at compact sizes. Avoid stripping the interface to plain text and monochrome controls.

Confirmed window widths: library and settings use 460 points; menu-bar popover and quick-paste HUD use 360 points. Keep these two width groups rather than making every surface equally wide.

History and settings share one 460-point main window with a persistent top page switcher: Settings on the left, Clipboard History on the right. Settings is one scrolling page with appearance, shortcuts, startup, privacy and sync cards; no internal category navigation. Page switching preserves settings drafts and history filters; never open a second settings window.

For individual settings controls, prefer simple, refined presentation: a compact input, subtle hover/recording highlight and one short hint. The user rejected decorative keyboard keycaps, pencil icons, extra dividers and repeated status text in the shortcut section.

### Design Principles
1. Balance content space with visual richness: compact brand headers and real status statistics, without oversized hero panels.
2. Keep navigation and primary actions stationary while only the content scrolls.
3. Use a 4-point spacing scale: 4/8 within related content, 12/16 between controls, 24 between sections.
4. Group settings by purpose and align labels, descriptions and controls consistently.
5. Preserve all existing actions, privacy safeguards and keyboard behavior.

History and settings use the persistent top tabs as their only page titles. Do not add duplicate headings, brand cards or status summaries inside either page. History begins with search; settings begins with its category tabs, leaving more room for content. Only the menu retains its summary header. Menu and quick paste share the main window background palette and compact action button style.

Top-level navigation uses an integrated background, no capsule container or divider, and a short animated underline for selection. Settings has no subgroup tabs. Appearance remains segmented to distinguish an option choice from navigation.

Startup and reopen are silent: only the menu-bar icon appears. Always run as an accessory app without a Dock icon. Open history/settings/popover only on explicit user navigation; no launch preference.

Main page tabs reuse the 28-point warm-to-blue ClipFlowBrandMark and rounded bold titles from the quick-paste header, retaining the integrated background and short underline.

Page switching keeps both pages mounted to retain scroll and draft state. Use a 0.16-second underline/icon response, a 0.26-second incoming content fade with 12-point directional motion, and a 0.12-second exit. Underline alignment follows the intrinsic icon/title group. Avoid artificial delays or queued callbacks; no replay on the current tab, no delayed animation queue, and no motion with Reduce Motion enabled. Hidden pages cannot receive interaction or accessibility focus.

Surface hierarchy: main settings/history cards use ultra-thin material without an extra white overlay, subtle borders and very light shadows (avoid milky gray surfaces); history rows and inputs have stronger opacity. Floating menu and quick-paste shells use slight material translucency (90% and 82% gradient tint), while text/images remain fully opaque. Honor Reduce Transparency with an opaque shell. Selected rows overlay accent on the stable base fill.

Approved app icon: assets/icon/ClipFlow-icon.svg, based on C Flow v11, shared warm-gold to blue gradient across both filled paths on a light palette tile. Menu-bar icon uses the same paths as a native monochrome template with pause bars when paused; menu header displays the bundled application icon.
